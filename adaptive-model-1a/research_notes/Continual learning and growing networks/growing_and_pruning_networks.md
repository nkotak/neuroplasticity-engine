## 1. What are the main 2024-2026 methods for growing networks during training, and what growth triggers and initialisation schemes do they use?

### Takeaway
Growth research in 2024-2026 falls into three families: function-preserving expansion of transformers (mostly on a fixed schedule, for cheaper pre-training or forgetting-free fine-tuning), trigger-driven neuron addition (saturation, loss-spike or bottleneck signals, shown only on small MLP/CNN settings), and "learned size" methods where a gradient-trained scalar sets the active width. The standard initialisation is still function-preserving (zero fan-out, or copy-and-rescale), but two independent results warn that preservation at initialisation does not predict final quality and that newly added units tend to be active in the forward pass yet starved of gradient.

### Cited Findings

**Function-preserving expansion of transformers and language models (Net2Net lineage)**
- "Stacking Your Transformers" (Du et al., submitted 24 May 2024, NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight) groups existing approaches into four atomic growth operators and finds a depth-wise stacking operator, G_stack, the best. Scale: LLMs up to 7B parameters after growth, pre-training up to 750B tokens. Against a conventionally trained 7B model using 300B tokens, the G_stack model reaches the same loss with 194B tokens, reported as a 54.6% speedup. The paper says it formalises guidelines for growth timing and growth factor (the guideline values were not on the abstract page). [abstract] — [arXiv:2405.15319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15319)
- "Landscape-Aware Growing: The Power of a Little LAG" (Karp, Saunshi, Miryoosefi, Reddi, Kumar; 4 June 2024) argues that a growth strategy (stacking, fusion and similar) should not be chosen by how well it preserves loss or function at initialisation, stating that "behavior at initialization can be misleading as a predictor of final performance"; early training dynamics after growth predict the best strategy better, and this motivates an adaptive schedule for gradual stacking. Transformer-based models; sizes not on the abstract page. [abstract] — [arXiv:2406.02469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02469)
- Masked Structural Growth (MSG; arXiv 2023, revised March 2024) claims growth operators that are strictly function-preserving in every growth dimension and independent of how new weights are initialised, for "2x faster" language-model pre-training by growing a small transformer into a larger one. [snippet] — [arXiv:2305.02869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02869)
- LOIRE (Han, Wang, Feng, Gao, Hu, Deng; China Mobile Research Institute; ICLR 2025) is a lifelong-learning framework that grows a pre-trained language model on incremental data. Its abstract criticises earlier growth work because some operators are not strictly function-preserving, some schedules cover only a few growth dimensions, and they assume new data matches the pre-training distribution (which causes forgetting). Its stated contributions are a plug-in layer growth operator (new layers are gated so they are skipped at first, keeping the function unchanged), multi-dimensional function-preserving operators, and a multi-stage growth procedure with distillation. [snippet; the proceedings PDF could not be parsed, so no numbers are reported here] — [ICLR 2025 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/30366); [proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/03945380ca5af0dc80782d8e0b72a8d7-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- "Grow, Don't Overwrite: Fine-tuning Without Forgetting" (Adila, Mazzawi, Dherin, Gonzalvo; Google Research; 9 March 2026) grows only the MLP (feed-forward) sublayers of a pre-trained transformer. The up-projection W(1) (h x p) is duplicated to h x 2p and the down-projection W(2) is stacked with each copy scaled by 1/2, so the expanded MLP's output equals the original at initialisation (proof given without bias terms; generalises to k copies scaled 1/k, the authors use k=2). New weights are copies of pre-trained weights; an ablation reports zero-initialised growth underperforms. Two variants: G-Freeze (default) trains only the new weights; G-Train trains the whole expanded up-projection and freezes the rest. [full-text] — [arXiv:2603.08647](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08647v1)
- Same paper, scale and results: main experiments on Gemma3-1B (Adam, lr 1e-3), with an appendix check on Gemma-4B. Retention is measured by a proxy (WinoGrande); new tasks are English-French translation (MTNT), SciTail, QASC and MathQA. Standard full fine-tuning's original-domain accuracy drops to "near-zero on tasks with large domain shifts", while G-Freeze stays "stable within a ±2%" range; new-task performance is described as "comparable or superior" to full fine-tuning (exact values only in figures). Function-vector analysis: entailment, full fine-tuning keeps 2/10 heads intact with similarity 0.28 versus 5/10 and 0.95 for the growth method; translation, 3/10 and 0.58 versus 5/10 and 0.76. Expanding all layers adds roughly 60% of the original parameter count; choosing layers by ranking weight-update magnitude from a preliminary fine-tuning run and growing the top 9-10 layers matched full expansion with trainable parameters around 30% of the model. No LoRA/PEFT baseline and no equal-size dense baseline are included. [full-text] — [arXiv:2603.08647](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08647v1)
- "Growing Transformers: Modular Composition and Layer-wise Expansion on a Frozen Substrate" (A. Bochkov; 8 July 2025, v3 2 May 2026) grows decoder-only transformers by stacking new blocks on a shallow model, training only the newest blocks and LM head, with previously trained blocks and the token interface frozen. A 16-layer, 269.7M-parameter model trained on 68.9B tokens reaches 28.92% MMLU after an interleaved LoRA stage. The abstract acknowledges "a clear tradeoff against dense monolithic training in final perplexity"; the 16-layer run has no same-size end-to-end control. [abstract] — [arXiv:2507.07129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07129)

**Trigger-driven neuron addition**
- "Growing Tiny Networks: Spotting Expressivity Bottlenecks and Fixing Them Optimally" (TINY; Verbockhaven, Chevallier, Charpiat, Rudkiewicz and co-authors; 30 May 2024, TMLR 2024) triggers growth from "expressivity bottlenecks when attempting to follow the functional gradient", extracted from backpropagation, and adds neurons chosen to fix the bottleneck. It claims to match large-network accuracy with competitive training time starting from very few neurons per layer, on CIFAR as a proof of concept. [abstract] — [arXiv:2405.19816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19816)
- NORACL (Raghunathan, Metzner, Kriener, Payvand; 29 April 2026, v2 16 July 2026) grows a layer only when two signals fire together (logical AND). Representational saturation: effective dimension (fraction of singular values of the normalised activation matrix above 0.05) exceeds gamma times its value at the end of the previous task (gamma = 0.9 on MNIST, 0.8 on Split CIFAR-10). Plasticity saturation: the p-th percentile of a fresh diagonal Fisher on current-task data exceeds an EMA (alpha = 0.9) of the layer's mean accumulated Fisher (p = 25 on MNIST, 50 on CIFAR-10). The number of neurons added scales with the size of the effective-dimension excess (minimum 1), followed by a 3-epoch cooldown. [full-text] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- NORACL initialisation: new neurons get fan-in weights from a QR-based orthogonal basis scaled by 0.2 and zero fan-out weights, so the function is unchanged at insertion; the optimiser is re-initialised after each growth event. Ablations: an effective-dimension-only trigger gives about 150k more parameters and 1.2% lower accuracy; a Fisher-only trigger gives 4.2% lower accuracy; heuristic triggers (fixed, scheduled, loss-based) are at least 1.9% worse; QR initialisation beats alternatives by at least 1.3%. [full-text] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- LACE (S. Tathe; 30 March 2026) expands when training loss exceeds 2.5 times a 50-step recent-loss baseline (a loss spike, signalling distribution shift, not a stall), with a 100-step warm-up and a 60-step cooldown; each expansion adds one dimension to a projection matrix, initialised from a normal distribution with sigma = 0.01 and activated through an output mask. It has no forgetting protection. [full-text] — [arXiv:2603.28611](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28611v1)
- "Width Expansion as a Method for Class Incremental Learning" (Conde, Elkhatib, Ranieri; 29 September 2026) adds neurons to existing layers based on a normalised loss criterion, without task identity at training or inference, combined with an attention mechanism with persistent key-value memory; tested on Split MNIST and Split CIFAR-100 with EWC, LwF and A-GEM. [abstract] — [arXiv:2609.37702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37702)
- "Shared-Weights Extender and Gradient Voting for Neural Network Expansion" (Chatzis, Kordonis, Theodosis, Maragos; 23 September 2025) addresses the problem that "newly added neurons often fail to adjust to a trained network" and stay inactive: the Shared-Weights Extender couples new neurons to existing ones, and a gradient-based Steepest Voting Distributor decides how many neurons each layer receives. Four datasets (not named on the abstract page). [abstract] — [arXiv:2509.18842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18842)
- "On the Stability of Growth in Structural Plasticity: Forward-Active yet Backward-Starved" (Lillo and Cheney, University of Vermont; arXiv ID indicates May 2026, v3 dated 3 October 2026) grows from a sparse seed at 5% compactness on a fixed cycle schedule, ranking dormant candidate units by how often their post-activation would exceed a small threshold; newborn units keep their original random initialisation and do not inherit optimiser state. Central finding: newborn units reach near-incumbent activation but receive substantially weaker gradients. Interventions on optimiser, insertion, selection and activation improve newborn integration but not necessarily final quality. [full-text, first 100k characters] — [arXiv:2605.15435](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15435)
- Neuroplastic Expansion (Liu, Obando-Ceron, Courville, Pan; 10 October 2024, v3 2 June 2025) grows a deep-RL network from about 20-25% of full size (starting sparsity 0.75) to full size on a fixed schedule (every 20k-50k steps, amount added decaying by cosine annealing), adding the candidate connections with the largest gradient magnitude, randomly initialised and weight-clipped. [full-text] — [arXiv:2410.07994](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07994v3)

**Learned-size methods (size is a gradient-trained quantity)**
- "Growing Neural Networks: Dynamic Evolution through Gradient Descent" (Radhakrishnan, Lindner, Miller, Sinha, Ditto; 29 January 2025; Proc. R. Soc. A 481(2318), 16 July 2025) uses either an auxiliary weight that acts as the size parameter (hidden neurons gated smoothly as it increases) or a controller-generated mask, with a size-loss term pulling size toward a target. There is no discrete trigger; growth is continuous. [full-text] — [arXiv:2501.18012](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18012v2)
- NGN, "Learning Neural Network Size as a Differentiable Count" (Lixing Li; 23 September 2026) gives each ordered component group one learnable boundary t; gate k is sigmoid(beta(t - k + 0.5)); the objective adds a capacity price (lambda t, annealed to zero) and an integer-snapping term. Candidate units are instantiated before training and zero-initialised; there is no explicit trigger, the boundary moves by gradient descent. Applied to MLPs, CNNs, graph networks, transformers (attention heads and FFN blocks), state-space models, LoRA and adapters. With hard forward gating, inactive frontier blocks get no weight updates until they activate, which the authors cite as a likely cause of stalling at small counts. [full-text] — [arXiv:2609.27291](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27291)

**Neuron splitting**
- "Expand Neurons, Not Parameters" (Kong, Subramanian, Shavit, Adler, Alistarh, Shavit; 6 October 2025, v3 3 June 2026, listed as accepted to ICML 2026) splits each neuron into several sparser sub-neurons so the network widens while the number of non-zero parameters stays fixed. It reports lower polysemanticity and higher accuracy on Boolean tasks, largest when feature interference is high, and consistent gains for CLIP-embedding classifiers, CNNs and deeper multilayer networks; random splits recover most of the gain. Effect sizes were not on the abstract page. [abstract] — [arXiv:2510.04500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04500)

**Mixture-of-experts and adapter expansion** (details in section 2)
- SEMA adds adapters on demand when representation descriptors detect a distribution shift; D-MoLE allocates LoRA experts layer-wise under a parameter budget; GainLoRA adds a gated LoRA branch per task. [abstract] — [arXiv:2403.18886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18886); [arXiv:2506.11672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11672); [arXiv:2505.15424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15424)

**Background (pre-2024, labelled as such)**
- Net2Net (Chen, Goodfellow, Shlens, ICLR 2016) introduced function-preserving widening and deepening; LAG states later transformer growth work mostly builds on it. [prior; lineage statement from snippet] — [arXiv:1511.05641](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05641); [arXiv:2406.02469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02469)
- GradMax (Evci et al., ICLR 2022) adds neurons without changing the output and picks new weights that maximise the gradient norm, computed via SVD. [snippet] — [arXiv:2201.05125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05125)
- Firefly (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2020) and splitting steepest descent (Liu, Wu, Wang, NeurIPS 2019) are the earlier gradient-based growth and neuron-splitting methods. [prior] — [arXiv:2102.08574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08574); [arXiv:1910.02366](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02366)
- Gesmundo and Maile (2023) gave composable function-preserving expansions for transformer architectures, a commonly cited base for the 2024-2026 transformer work. [prior] — [arXiv:2308.06103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06103)
- The "Growing Wiki" describes itself as the first methodological overview of growing architectures during training and notes that prior surveys focused on pruning or dynamic inference. [snippet] — [growingnet.github.io](https://growingnet.github.io/growing_wiki/overview/index.html)

### Inferences
- The planned experiment (tiny transformer adds feed-forward neurons when a task stalls, protects old neurons, prunes unused ones) has close but partial precedents: "Grow, Don't Overwrite" covers FFN-only growth with frozen old weights in a transformer but uses a fixed one-shot doubling, not a trigger; NORACL covers on-demand triggering and protection but only in MLPs; NGN covers FFN-block growth in a 2-layer TinyStories transformer but with no continual-learning setting. No paper found combines all three elements in a transformer.
- Triggers split into "shift detectors" (loss spike in LACE, descriptor shift in SEMA) and "saturation detectors" (effective dimension plus Fisher in NORACL, expressivity bottleneck in TINY). A "new task stalls" trigger is a loss-plateau heuristic; NORACL's ablation is the only direct evidence found on that class of trigger, and it was at least 1.9% worse than the saturation trigger in their MLP setting.
- Zero fan-out (or copy-and-halve) initialisation is the safe default for not disturbing old-task outputs, but the 2605.15435 and 2509.18842 results imply that zero or weak coupling can leave new neurons under-trained; resetting or separately handling optimiser state for new units and a post-growth cooldown (NORACL 3 epochs, LACE 60 steps) are recurring engineering details worth copying.
- "Grow, Don't Overwrite" found copying existing weights beat zero-initialised growth in a pre-trained LLM, while NORACL found orthogonal fan-in with zero fan-out best in small MLPs; the best initialisation appears setting-dependent, so the experiment should treat it as an ablation axis.

### Gaps
- No direct 2024-2026 successor to GradMax or Firefly (gradient-maximising initialisation of new neurons) was found; searches returned only the original GradMax paper. This may be a search-coverage gap.
- LOIRE's quantitative claims (compute reduction, forgetting) could not be read; the PDF was not machine-readable in this session.
- The G_stack growth-timing and growth-factor guidelines, TINY's exact initialisation formula and scale, and the dataset names for SWE/SVoD were not on the abstract pages.
- No 2024-2026 paper on mid-network layer insertion (as opposed to stacking at the top or gated plug-in layers) was verified.
- "Dynamic Mixture of Experts Against Severe Distribution Shifts" (Donghu Kim, 24 November 2025, [arXiv:2511.18987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18987)) evaluates a DynamicMoE approach against network-expansion methods in continual and reinforcement learning; its mechanism and results were not on the abstract page.

## 2. What are the dynamic-architecture approaches to continual learning, what do they report versus fixed-capacity baselines, and how does parameter count scale with the number of tasks?

### Takeaway
The centre of gravity has moved from growing whole networks (Progressive Networks, DEN, CPG) to adding small modules to a frozen pre-trained backbone: a LoRA branch or adapter per task (linear growth), on-demand adapters (claimed sub-linear), or budgeted mixtures of LoRA experts. Neuron-level growth with protection survives mainly in small-scale work (NORACL, width expansion, GRACE), where reported gains over fixed-capacity baselines are a few accuracy points and benchmark-dependent.

### Cited Findings

**Expandable LoRA / adapter modules on pre-trained models**
- GainLoRA (Liang, Chen, Li; 21 May 2025, NeurIPS 2025) "expands a new LoRA branch for each new task" and uses gating modules to integrate new and old branches so that the new branch's influence on old tasks is minimised; it claims to outperform existing state-of-the-art continual-learning methods for LLMs (no numbers on the abstract page). [abstract] — [arXiv:2505.15424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15424)
- A search summary of the same line of work states that most such methods add a new LoRA branch per task while freezing old branches to mitigate forgetting. [snippet] — [arXiv:2505.15424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15424)
- SEMA, "Self-Expansion of Pre-trained Models with Mixture of Adapters" (Wang, Lu, Yao, Gong; 27 March 2024, v3 27 March 2025) reuses or adds adapters "on demand": each adapter carries a representation descriptor trained as a distribution-shift indicator, and a shift that existing adapters cannot handle fires expansion; an expandable router mixes adapter outputs. It claims a "sub-linear expansion rate" versus linear growth for per-task modules and state-of-the-art results among rehearsal-free pre-trained-model methods. No parameter curves on the abstract page. [abstract] — [arXiv:2403.18886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18886)
- D-MoLE (Ge, Wang, Zhang, Chen, Fan, Huang, Xue, Zhu; 13 June 2025, ICML 2025) uses a "dynamic layer-wise expert allocator" to place LoRA experts across layers under a controlled parameter budget for continual multimodal instruction tuning, and reports "a 15% average improvement over the best baseline". [abstract] — [arXiv:2506.11672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11672)
- LiLoRA (2025) shares LoRA components across tasks while keeping a task-specific low-rank decomposition, arguing that expanding entire backbone layers per task "quickly becomes inefficient due to significant parameter redundancy". C-LoRA (2025) introduces a module per task and merges it into the previous one; FM-LoRA (2025) uses dynamic rank selection to reduce the burden of managing many adapters. [snippet] — [arXiv:2508.06202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06202); [arXiv:2502.17920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17920); [arXiv:2504.08823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08823)

**Neuron- or backbone-level growth with protection**
- NORACL protects old knowledge with online EWC (quadratic penalty weighted by an EMA of the Fisher diagonal), not freezing; new parameters get zero Fisher and zero penalty. EWC lambda: 500 (Permuted MNIST), 2000 (Rotated MNIST), 5000 (Binary Split MNIST and Split CIFAR-10). All benchmarks are domain-incremental with a single shared head. [full-text] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- NORACL versus fixed-width EWC networks, final average accuracy over 5 seeds (static small / medium / large versus NORACL, with parameter counts): Permuted MNIST 1 hidden layer, 44.0 ± 0.9 (12.7k) / 60.3 ± 1.9 (25.4k) / 76.0 ± 0.8 (50.8k) versus 79.9 ± 0.5 (47.6k ± 1.6k); Permuted MNIST 2 layers, 41.9 ± 1.3 / 56.5 ± 1.1 / 73.3 ± 1.8 (54.9k) versus 79.4 ± 0.7 (49.2k ± 3.2k); Rotated MNIST 1 layer, 56.3 ± 1.7 / 63.7 ± 2.3 / 73.6 ± 1.2 (50.8k) versus 72.6 ± 2.4 (42.2k ± 2.1k); Rotated MNIST 2 layers, 56.1 ± 1.4 / 63.3 ± 1.7 / 75.2 ± 0.8 (54.9k) versus 74.9 ± 1.7 (42.9k ± 2.7k); Binary Split MNIST 1 layer, 71.6 ± 1.9 / 72.8 ± 0.9 (25.4k) / 70.0 ± 2.7 versus 72.1 ± 1.8 (23.8k ± 2.2k); Binary Split MNIST 2 layers, 72.1 ± 1.8 / 71.2 ± 2.6 / 72.5 ± 1.8 versus 73.9 ± 2.5 (23.3k ± 3.1k). [full-text] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- NORACL parameter scaling depends on task geometry, not just task count: about 49k (Permuted), 43k (Rotated) and 23k (Binary Split) parameters for the 2-layer network. Dissimilar tasks (Permuted MNIST) grow layer 1 from about 32 to about 60 neurons over 10 tasks while layer 2 stays flat; highly similar tasks (Binary Split) grow layer 2 faster than layer 1 (about 36 versus 23). The paper's stated limitations: MLPs only, small benchmarks, EWC as the only consolidation backbone, and about 48% absolute accuracy on a 50-task Permuted MNIST stress test. [full-text] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- GRACE, "Grow, Assess, Compress" (Garcia-Castañeda, Irureta, Imaz, Lojo; 9 March 2026) cycles backbone expansion, a saturation assessment of capacity use, and compression for class-incremental learning; it claims state-of-the-art results with memory footprint reduced by "up to a 73%" relative to purely expansionist models. [abstract] — [arXiv:2603.08426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08426)
- "Width Expansion as a Method for Class Incremental Learning" (29 September 2026) reports that progressive width expansion consistently beats fixed architectures on Split MNIST and Split CIFAR-100 in class-incremental learning, especially with functional regularisation methods and A-GEM, and warns that uncontrolled growth may increase overfitting and compute. [abstract] — [arXiv:2609.37702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37702)
- "Grow, Don't Overwrite" G-Freeze is a freeze-old, train-new scheme in a transformer: retention within ±2% on the proxy benchmark versus near-zero for full fine-tuning on large-shift tasks, at a cost of roughly 60% more parameters for full expansion (one new task; multi-task sequential scaling is not reported). [full-text] — [arXiv:2603.08647](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08647v1)
- LACE adds capacity on loss spikes but states it addresses when to add capacity, not how to prevent forgetting; it has no replay or regularisation. [full-text] — [arXiv:2603.28611](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28611v1)
- Under repeated-shift benchmarks (Permuted MNIST, Random Label MNIST/CIFAR, CIFAR 5+1, Continual ImageNet) and class-incremental splits, Lillo and Cheney find Prune is generally the strongest or near-strongest structural method, with Grow competitive but more regime-sensitive; a plasticity-preserving activation (Rand. Smooth-Leaky) makes Grow the most reliable growth variant on Split-CIFAR100. They also note, citing Zhao et al. 2024, that "repeated expansion can itself destabilize learning". [full-text, first 100k characters; the Zhao et al. citation was not independently checked] — [arXiv:2605.15435](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15435)

**Sparse-subnetwork / supermask methods within fixed capacity**
- PALL (Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning, 2025) optimises task-specific sparse subnetworks with parameter sharing inside a single fixed-capacity architecture and freezes parameters to prevent forgetting. Sparse Subnetwork Distillation (December 2025) combines sparse subnetworks with knowledge distillation. [snippet] — [arXiv:2505.10941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10941); [arXiv:2512.15267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15267)
- Searches found no single prominent 2024-2025 "PackNet successor"; the mask-based line (ESPN 2022, ExSSNeT 2022, SparCL 2022) predates the window. [snippet] — [arXiv:2210.10209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10209); [arXiv:2203.02026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02026)

**Fixed-capacity side of the comparison**
- "On the Diminishing Returns of Width for Continual Learning" (Guha and Lakshman; 11 March 2024, ICML 2024) states the authors "prove that width is directly related to forgetting in Feed-Forward Networks" and that increasing width to reduce forgetting "yields diminishing returns", confirmed empirically at widths not explored before. [abstract] — [arXiv:2403.06398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06398)

**Background (pre-2024, labelled as such)**
- Progressive Neural Networks (2016) add a new column per task with lateral connections and freeze old columns, so parameters grow at least linearly with tasks; DEN (2018) selectively retrains, expands and splits/duplicates units; PackNet (2018) prunes after each task and freezes surviving weights inside a fixed network; CPG (2019) combines compaction, picking of old weights and growth; SupSup (2020) learns a per-task supermask over a fixed random network. [prior] — [arXiv:1606.04671](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04671); [arXiv:1708.01547](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01547); [arXiv:1711.05769](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05769); [arXiv:1910.06562](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06562); [arXiv:2006.14769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14769)

### Inferences
- Parameter scaling regimes observed in 2024-2026 work: linear in tasks (per-task LoRA branch plus gate, as in GainLoRA and C-LoRA); claimed sub-linear (SEMA, expansion only on detected shift); budget-capped (D-MoLE); demand-driven and geometry-dependent (NORACL, roughly 2x between similar-task and dissimilar-task streams); and grow-then-compress (GRACE).
- Old-knowledge protection comes in two styles: hard freezing of old parameters or modules (G-Freeze, per-task LoRA, PackNet-style masks), which gives near-zero forgetting but no backward transfer, and soft regularisation of old parameters (NORACL with EWC), which lets old neurons adapt. The planned experiment's "protect neurons used by old tasks" could be either; the two have not been compared head-to-head in the growth papers found.
- Evidence versus fixed-capacity baselines is strongest when the fixed network is under-provisioned (NORACL versus static small/medium) and weak or within error bars when the fixed network is similar in size (see section 4).
- Guha and Lakshman's result implies a wider fixed network is a strong but saturating baseline for forgetting; a growth method should be compared against it at several widths, not one.

### Gaps
- Benchmarks, base models and numbers for GainLoRA, SEMA and D-MoLE (beyond the 15% claim) were not on the abstract pages; SEMA's sub-linear claim could not be checked.
- No verified 2024-2026 paper reports how total parameters scale over long task sequences (for example 50+ tasks) for transformer neuron-level growth; NORACL's 50-task test is MLP-only.
- Whether the width-expansion paper (2609.37702) and GRACE match parameters against their fixed baselines is not stated on the abstract pages.
- PALL and SSD results were seen only in search summaries.

## 3. What is the state of dynamic sparse training for transformers and language models, and has it been applied to continual learning or to preventing loss of plasticity?

### Takeaway
Dynamic sparse training (prune-and-regrow of connections at a fixed parameter budget) now has LLM-scale results: the Cannistraci-Hebb soft-rule method reports near-dense perplexity at 70% sparsity up to a 1B-parameter LLaMA, while RigL performs poorly in that setting. Applications to continual learning and plasticity exist but are small-scale: a task-incremental CNN study, a multi-task RL study showing SET and gradual pruning reduce dormancy, and an RL method that grows a sparse network to full size while pruning dormant neurons.

### Cited Findings

**DST for transformers and LLMs**
- "Brain network science modelling of sparse neural networks enables Transformers and LLMs to perform as fully connected" (Zhang, Cerretti, Zhao, Liao, Wu, Michieli, Cannistraci; arXiv January 2025, v4 13 January 2026; a search summary says accepted to NeurIPS 2025) proposes CHTs and CHTss. Removal samples links from a multinomial over a mix of weight magnitude and relative importance; regrowth uses a gradient-free, node-based topological link predictor (CH2-L3n) with soft sampling at O(N^3); CHTss adds a sigmoid-based gradual density decay. [full-text, first 100k characters; venue from snippet] — [arXiv:2501.19107](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19107v4)
- Same paper, LLaMA on OpenWebText, validation perplexity (lower is better): LLaMA-60M dense 26.56, CHTss 27.62 at 70% sparsity and 35.10 at 95%, CHTs 28.12 at 70%, GraNet 30.55 and RigL 39.96 at 70%. LLaMA-130M dense 19.27, CHTss 19.85 at 70% and 25.07 at 95%, CHTs 20.10 and GMP 20.49 at 70%. LLaMA-1B (70% sparsity only) dense 14.62, CHTs 14.53, CHTss 15.41, SET 16.37, GMP 31.76, RigL 149.17. The paper attributes RigL's and GraNet's weak results partly to bfloat16 precision. [full-text] — [arXiv:2501.19107](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19107v4)
- Same paper, machine translation (BLEU): CHTss exceeds the dense model at 95% sparsity on Multi30k (32.03) and IWSLT (24.51); CHTs trails RigL on Multi30k (28.94 versus 29.94 at 95%) but beats RigL and SET on IWSLT and WMT (for example IWSLT 95%: 21.15 versus RigL 20.53, SET 18.53). MLP on CIFAR-10 at 99% sparsity: dense 62.85%, CHTss 71.29%, CHTs 69.97%, GraNet 69.31%, GMP 65.11%, RigL 63.90%, SET 62.70%. The dense row of the translation table was garbled in extraction and is not reported. [full-text] — [arXiv:2501.19107](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19107v4)
- Mixed Sparsity Training (2024) combines DST with a sparsity-variation schedule for transformer pre-training and reports about 75% reduction in floating-point operations while maintaining performance. [snippet] — [arXiv:2408.11746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11746)
- "Always-Sparse Training by Growing Connections with Guided Stochastic Exploration" (January 2024) positions itself as avoiding RigL's periodic dense-gradient computation. [snippet] — [arXiv:2401.06898](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06898v2)
- SparseOpt (May 2026) targets normalisation-induced gradient skew in sparse training, uses RigL as a baseline, and describes DST as widely considered the state-of-the-art sparse-training approach. [snippet] — [arXiv:2605.27541](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27541)
- Background caveat: the Gopher scaling paper (2021) reported minimal FLOP reduction to reach a given performance when training large language models with RigL. [snippet] — [arXiv:2112.11446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11446)

**DST applied to continual learning**
- "Continual Learning with Dynamic Sparse Training: Exploring Algorithms for Effective Model Updates" (Yildirim, Gok Yildirim, Sokar, Mocanu, Vanschoren; 28 August 2023, v2 4 December 2023) studies DST components in task-incremental learning on CIFAR-100 and miniImageNet: at low sparsity, ERK initialisation uses the backbone more efficiently; at high sparsity, uniform initialisation is more reliable "unless it is extreme"; the best growth strategy depends on initialisation and sparsity, with no single winner. [abstract; published just before the 2024 window] — [arXiv:2308.14831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14831)
- Lillo and Cheney (2026) compare growing a sparse mask against iterative magnitude pruning under continual shift; Prune is generally strongest, Grow competitive (details in sections 2 and 4). [full-text] — [arXiv:2605.15435](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15435)

**DST and loss of plasticity**
- "Sparsity-Driven Plasticity in Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning" (Todorov, Cardenas-Cartagena, Cunha, Zullich, Sabatelli; TMLR 2025, arXiv 9 August 2025) finds Gradual Magnitude Pruning and Sparse Evolutionary Training mitigate plasticity-degradation indicators (neuron dormancy, representational collapse), often correlating with better multi-task performance; sparse agents frequently outperform dense ones and are competitive with explicit plasticity interventions. The authors call dynamic sparsification robust but context-sensitive. RigL is not named on the abstract page. [abstract] — [arXiv:2508.06871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06871)
- Neuroplastic Expansion (2024-2025) combines three pieces in TD3 and DrQ agents: gradient-magnitude topology growth from a sparse start, pruning of dormant neurons (output exactly zero; per-layer cap equal to 0.2-0.4 times the number of newly grown elements; pruned neurons are re-initialised and can be regrown), and "experience review" that samples from the earliest quarter of the replay buffer when the activated-neuron count is changing slowly. Benchmarks: MuJoCo (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid; 3M-6M steps), a cyclic continual-adaptation sequence, and image-based DeepMind Control. Baselines: Reset, ReDo, layer normalisation, Plasticity Injection. Reported ablation: Ant end-of-training return 7749.25 ± 127.73 with experience review versus 7235.14 ± 493.83 without (10 seeds). [full-text] — [arXiv:2410.07994](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07994v3)
- Background within window: Dohare et al. (Nature, 2024) show standard deep-learning methods gradually lose plasticity in continual-learning settings until they learn no better than a shallow network; their remedy, continual backpropagation, re-initialises a small fraction of less-used units, a fixed-size analogue of neuron turnover. [snippet for the first claim; mechanism from prior knowledge] — [Nature 2024 via PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11338828/)

### Inferences
- DST and neuron-level growth answer different questions: DST keeps the parameter count fixed and moves connections; growth raises the count. The planned "prune unused neurons" step is closest to dormant-neuron pruning in Neuroplastic Expansion and to continual backpropagation's unit replacement, both of which treat inactivity as the pruning criterion.
- RigL's gradient-based regrowth appears fragile at LLM scale in the one detailed comparison found (149.17 perplexity at 1B versus 14.62 dense), while gradient-free topological regrowth and even random regrowth (SET, 16.37) did far better; if the experiment adds connection-level regrowth, a SET-style baseline is cheap and credible. This rests on one paper whose authors propose the winning method.
- No evidence was found that DST has been used for continual learning in language models; the continual-learning DST evidence is vision (CNN) and RL only.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 paper was found that applies RigL/SET-style DST to continual learning of a transformer or language model.
- The CHTs paper's later sections (beyond the first 100k characters) were not read; whether it mentions continual learning is unknown. Its NeurIPS 2025 status comes from a search summary only.
- An August 2025 selective-weight-reinitialisation paper reportedly lists DST-plus-plasticity as future work, but its arXiv ID could not be confirmed, so it is not cited.
- Mixed Sparsity Training, Always-Sparse Training and SparseOpt were not opened; their numbers are from search summaries.

## 4. Is there published evidence, positive or negative, that growing capacity on demand beats starting with a larger fixed network of the same final size?

### Takeaway
The evidence is mixed and thin. Clean size-matched controls are rare; where growth wins (tiny regression networks, NORACL on some benchmarks, Neuroplastic Expansion in RL) the setting is small or non-stationary and margins are modest, and the cleanest matched-size, matched-budget test (NGN, including a 2-layer transformer) finds fixed networks equal or slightly better. Several results suggest any advantage of growth comes from the training trajectory under non-stationarity, not from a better final architecture.

### Cited Findings

**Positive evidence**
- Radhakrishnan et al. (2025) state growing networks "consistently outperform static networks of equivalent final size" on nonlinear regression and classification. Scale: target sizes of 5 and 10 hidden neurons. Bessel-function regression, 200 trials: mean final static loss is about 5 times the mean final growing loss. Spiral classification and composite-Bessel regression, 100 trials: growing networks reach comparable accuracy with about half the neurons (A^g_N ≈ A^s_2N); no other numbers given. The advantage requires slow growth over long training and a tuned size-loss weight; the authors hypothesise reduced susceptibility to local minima but do not test it. Only small feed-forward networks were studied. [full-text] — [arXiv:2501.18012](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18012v2)
- NORACL includes an explicit size-matched control on Split CIFAR-10 (frozen ResNet18 features, MLP head): static 172x134 provisioned at NORACL's maximum size from the start, 111k parameters, 85.4 ± 0.8%; NORACL grown from 128x128, 105k ± 5.7k parameters, 86.3 ± 1.1%. Other static widths: 128x128 (82.2k) 85.0 ± 1.9; 256x256 (197k) 85.2 ± 0.9; 512x512 (525k) 85.3 ± 1.2; 1024x1024 (1.5M) 84.7 ± 0.3. The difference to the size-matched control is within the reported standard deviations. The reader noted the table caption says "55 seeds" while the text says 5 seeds. [full-text] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- NORACL on MNIST variants versus a similar-size static EWC network: clearly better on Permuted MNIST (79.9 ± 0.5 at 47.6k versus 76.0 ± 0.8 at 50.8k; 79.4 ± 0.7 at 49.2k versus 73.3 ± 1.8 at 54.9k), slightly worse within error on Rotated MNIST (72.6 ± 2.4 versus 73.6 ± 1.2; 74.9 ± 1.7 versus 75.2 ± 0.8). On a 50-task Permuted MNIST stress test it overtakes a static 75x32 network matched to its maximum final size after fewer than 10 tasks and finishes highest, at about 48% accuracy. The abstract's summary is that accuracy is "better than or on par with oracle-provisioned static baselines while using fewer parameters". [full-text; abstract] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- Neuroplastic Expansion compares against static full-size TD3 networks with its own final size matched to them, and reports it "significantly outperforms the original TD3" in continual adaptation; numeric scores for the static baseline appear only in figures. [full-text] — [arXiv:2410.07994](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07994v3)
- G_stack: a grown 7B LLM reaches the same loss as a conventionally trained 7B model with 194B instead of 300B tokens (54.6% speedup). This is a compute-to-loss advantage at equal final size in stationary pre-training, not an on-demand or continual-learning result. [abstract] — [arXiv:2405.15319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15319)
- TINY claims to match large-network accuracy with competitive training time starting from very few neurons (CIFAR proof of concept). [abstract] — [arXiv:2405.19816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19816)
- The width-expansion class-incremental paper reports progressive expansion consistently beats fixed architectures (whether parameter-matched is not stated). [abstract] — [arXiv:2609.37702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37702)

**Negative or neutral evidence**
- NGN retrains fixed models at the learned size (ten seeds): MNIST MLP 96.94 ± 0.07 (NGN) versus 96.81 ± 0.09 (fixed); MNIST conv 99.17 ± 0.03 versus 99.14 ± 0.04; CIFAR-10 conv 71.74 ± 0.26 versus 72.08 ± 0.29; Cora GCN 78.68 ± 1.66 versus 79.43 ± 1.18. In a controlled 1D study (50 seeds) fixed models are "typically slightly better at matched retained count". [full-text] — [arXiv:2609.27291](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27291)
- NGN on 2-layer transformers, TinyStories, bits per character (lower is better, single seed; truncated NGN versus fixed at the same size): attention heads 1.3234 versus 1.3011; FFN blocks 1.3775 versus 1.3402; heads plus FFN blocks 1.3902 versus 1.3270; Mamba state dimension 1.3597 versus 1.3445. The author states the benefit is "automatic size selection, not faster optimization or consistently better performance at the selected size", and that NGN needs more optimisation steps. [full-text] — [arXiv:2609.27291](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27291)
- LACE (accuracy; dimensions): 10 synthetic domains, LACE 0.999 (73 final dims) versus Fixed-Large 0.999 (84) and Fixed-Small 0.998 (64); 50 domains, LACE 0.676 (38 final dims, 30 expansions) versus Fixed-Large 0.884 (48) and Fixed-Small 0.434 (8); real-world sequence on frozen GPT-2 embeddings, LACE 0.796 (34 dims) versus Fixed-Large 0.821 (128) and Fixed-Small 0.667 (32). Fixed-Large is always larger than LACE's final size, so this is not a size-matched control; the reader also flagged an internal inconsistency (13% versus about 19% fewer dimensions). Single-author preprint. [full-text] — [arXiv:2603.28611](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28611v1)
- Lillo and Cheney, CIFAR-100 ConvNet at 50% compactness, in-place accuracy: Grow 54.3%, Prune 50.2%, Dense (full unedited architecture) 49.7%; but when each final mask is rewound and retrained from scratch, Grow and Prune are "indistinguishable". ResNet all-layer editing: Grow 58.5%, Prune 58.0%; retrained 59.2% and 59.0%. Their conclusion: Grow's advantage during editing comes from the growth trajectory, not a better final sparse architecture. [full-text] — [arXiv:2605.15435](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15435)
- Same paper, adaptability after growth ("future reachability"): head-only growth adapts faster early (about 25% after 500 steps versus 19% dense and 16% prune; all end near 51%), but growth across all residual layers ends lower (41.9% Grow, 46.2% Prune, 51.3% Dense). [full-text] — [arXiv:2605.15435](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15435)
- "Growing Transformers" on a frozen substrate concedes "a clear tradeoff against dense monolithic training in final perplexity". [abstract] — [arXiv:2507.07129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07129)
- LAG: function-preserving initialisation, the usual justification for "growth costs nothing", "can be misleading as a predictor of final performance". [abstract] — [arXiv:2406.02469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02469)
- "Grow, Don't Overwrite" includes no dense baseline at the expanded size; its comparison is against fine-tuning the original-size model. [full-text] — [arXiv:2603.08647](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08647v1)
- Guha and Lakshman (ICML 2024): widening a fixed feed-forward network reduces forgetting with diminishing returns, which bounds how much a bigger fixed baseline can gain. [abstract] — [arXiv:2403.06398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06398)

**Related but different control**
- "Expand Neurons, Not Parameters" holds non-zero parameter count fixed and widens by splitting; it reports consistent accuracy gains from more, sparser neurons at equal parameters. This tests width versus density at fixed parameters, not growth-during-training versus fixed size. [abstract] — [arXiv:2510.04500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04500)

### Inferences
- No paper found runs the exact control the planned experiment needs: a transformer that grows FFN neurons on demand during a task sequence, versus the same transformer started at the final width, with the same forgetting-protection method, the same total training steps, and multiple seeds. NORACL (MLP, EWC) and NGN (stationary, 2-layer transformer) are the nearest halves of it.
- The pattern across results: in stationary training with matched size and budget, growth does not improve final accuracy (NGN; Lillo and Cheney's retrain-from-scratch test) and may cost steps; under non-stationarity, growth sometimes helps (NORACL Permuted MNIST, Neuroplastic Expansion), plausibly because fresh units restore plasticity or because a large network regularised by EWC from the start wastes or stiffens capacity. This mechanism is a hypothesis; NORACL's own size-matched CIFAR-10 control is within noise.
- Expected effect sizes are small: 0.9 points on the only size-matched NORACL control, 4-6 points on Permuted MNIST versus a slightly larger static network, and negative 0.02-0.06 bits per character for NGN transformers. An experiment needs enough seeds to resolve differences of about 1 accuracy point.
- The fixed baseline must receive the same protection mechanism. NORACL's static baselines all use EWC; a fixed-large network with per-task neuron freezing (PackNet-style allocation of pre-existing neurons) is the fairest comparator for "protect neurons used by old tasks" and was not found tested against on-demand growth in any 2024-2026 paper.
- Reporting should separate (a) final accuracy at equal parameters, (b) compute to reach it, and (c) parameters actually used, because published wins are mostly on (b) or (c) (G_stack, NORACL's parameter savings, NGN's size selection) and not on (a).

### Gaps
- Neuroplastic Expansion's numeric margin over the size-matched dense TD3 baseline is only in figures and was not extracted.
- No size-matched growth-versus-fixed comparison for language-model continual learning was found.
- The Zhao et al. 2024 result cited by Lillo and Cheney ("repeated expansion can itself destabilize learning"; a search summary paraphrases it as expansion inducing forgetting in task-agnostic settings) was not located or verified.
- Whether TINY and the width-expansion paper match parameter counts and training budgets against their fixed baselines could not be determined from abstracts.

## 5. Are there biologically inspired 2024-2026 papers on neurogenesis or structural plasticity in artificial networks, and what do they actually demonstrate?

### Takeaway
Yes, but the demonstrations are small-scale and the biology is mostly a framing. The most concrete results are NORACL (saturation-triggered neurogenesis in MLPs on MNIST and CIFAR-10 features), Neuroplastic Expansion (growth plus dormant-neuron pruning in MuJoCo/DMC agents), the forward-active-yet-backward-starved finding about newborn units, and brain-network-inspired sparse rewiring that scales to a 1B LLaMA; none shows neurogenesis improving continual learning in a transformer.

### Cited Findings
- NORACL frames fixed-capacity continual learners as needing an "oracle" architecture sized for an unknown future task stream and proposes neurogenesis-inspired growth from a compact network. It demonstrates, on 1-2 hidden-layer ReLU MLPs (domain-incremental Permuted/Rotated/Binary Split MNIST, Split CIFAR-10 on frozen features, 5-10 tasks plus a 50-task stress test), accuracy on par with or above static EWC networks at similar or lower parameter counts, and that where neurons are added depends on task similarity (early layers for dissimilar tasks, later layers for similar ones). Its theory covers a single-hidden-layer random-pattern setting with MSE loss. Stated limits: MLPs only; convolutional and attention models left to future work. [full-text] — [arXiv:2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27031)
- Lillo and Cheney study "structural plasticity" as growing versus pruning and demonstrate that newborn units are "forward-active yet backward-starved": near-incumbent activations, substantially weaker gradients. With ReLU, newborn gradients are low at birth; with a randomised smooth-leaky activation, post-birth gradients move toward parity. Architectures: 3-layer MLP (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST), ConvNet with editable FC head and CIFAR-style ResNet (CIFAR-100). [full-text, first 100k characters] — [arXiv:2605.15435](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15435)
- Neuroplastic Expansion (Liu, Obando-Ceron, Courville, Pan) demonstrates, in TD3 and DrQ agents on MuJoCo and DeepMind Control, that growing a network from about a quarter of its size to full size while pruning dormant neurons and replaying early experience mitigates plasticity loss and outperforms Reset, ReDo, layer normalisation and Plasticity Injection baselines (per the authors; RL only). [full-text] — [arXiv:2410.07994](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07994v3)
- SERENA (arXiv 2024, revised June 2025, CoLLAs 2025) borrows self-regulated neurogenesis: each concept is assigned a specialised network path, and once learned its "concept cell" is frozen to prevent forgetting. [snippet] — [arXiv:2403.14684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14684v2)
- "Neuroplasticity in Artificial Intelligence" (Li, Milling, Schuller; March 2025) is a survey/perspective on neurogenesis, neuroapoptosis and neuroplasticity in deep learning; it proposes "dropin" as the artificial analogue of neurogenesis and treats dropout and structural pruning as analogues of neuroapoptosis. It is a conceptual overview, not an experimental demonstration. [snippet] — [arXiv:2503.21419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21419)
- NGN ("Neurogenesis Network", September 2026) uses the neurogenesis label for a learnable active-prefix boundary; it demonstrates automatic size selection across many architectures but no accuracy gain over fixed-size training, and its transformer results are single-seed. [full-text] — [arXiv:2609.27291](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27291)
- Cannistraci-Hebb training (CHTs/CHTss) is inspired by brain network science: connections are regrown by a gradient-free topological link-prediction rule. It demonstrates sparse transformers and LLaMA models (60M, 130M, 1B) approaching or matching dense performance at 70-95% sparsity; this is connection rewiring at fixed size, not neuron addition, and not a continual-learning result. [full-text] — [arXiv:2501.19107](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19107v4)
- Dohare et al. (Nature 2024) show loss of plasticity under standard deep learning in continual settings and propose continual backpropagation, which keeps plasticity by re-initialising a small fraction of less-used units, an artificial analogue of neuron turnover at fixed network size. [snippet; mechanism from prior knowledge] — [Nature 2024 via PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11338828/)
- Background (2018): "On the role of neurogenesis in overcoming catastrophic forgetting" reported that structural plasticity helps prevent catastrophic forgetting in non-stationary settings and is cited as a foundation for the 2025 work. [snippet] — [arXiv:1811.02113](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02113)

### Inferences
- The biologically inspired papers converge on a three-part recipe that matches the planned experiment: add units when existing ones saturate, consolidate or freeze what old tasks use, and remove or recycle inactive units. Each part has separate small-scale support (NORACL for triggered addition, EWC/freezing for consolidation, Neuroplastic Expansion and continual backpropagation for recycling dormant units), but no single paper found validates all three together outside RL.
- The "backward-starved newborn" result is the most actionable biological-plasticity finding for implementation: after adding FFN neurons, log per-neuron gradient norms for new versus old units, and consider a non-saturating activation or a temporary learning-rate boost for new units.
- Transformer-scale neurogenesis for continual learning is an open gap as of November 2026: NORACL lists attention models as future work, and the only transformer neuron-growth results found (NGN, "Grow, Don't Overwrite") are not continual-learning neurogenesis studies.

### Gaps
- SERENA and the Li-Milling-Schuller survey were seen only in search summaries; their experiments and claims were not verified.
- No verified 2024-2026 paper models neuron death/pruning and birth jointly in a transformer under a task sequence.
- No neuroscience-grounded quantitative comparison (for example matching biological neurogenesis rates) was found; the biological link in these papers is motivational.
- Papers from June to November 2026 are covered only as far as web search surfaced them (NORACL v2, NGN, width expansion, Lillo and Cheney v3); coverage of that period is likely incomplete.
