## 1. What do 2024-2026 theory papers say about when and why forgetting happens?

### Takeaway
Forgetting is not a monotone function of any single factor: the 2024-2026 theory (mostly continual linear regression, teacher-student models and infinite-width limits) says it depends jointly on task similarity, the degree of overparameterization, how much feature learning occurs (lazy versus rich regime), task order, and step size. The most transferable results are that intermediate task similarity is worst in highly overparameterized models, that width helps only insofar as it makes training lazier (and with diminishing returns), and that random task ordering alone drives forgetting to zero in long sequences of jointly realizable linear tasks.

### Cited Findings

**Task similarity and overparameterization**
- [S] Goldfarb, Evron, Weinberger, Soudry and Hand (arXiv 2401.12617, January 2024, ICLR 2024) study two-task continual linear regression where the second task is a random orthogonal transformation of an arbitrary first task (an abstraction of permuted-input benchmarks) and derive an exact expression for expected forgetting. In highly overparameterized models intermediate task similarity causes the most forgetting; near the interpolation threshold forgetting decreases monotonically with expected task similarity. Checked on synthetic linear regression and on fully connected networks on permuted-image benchmarks. — [arXiv 2401.12617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12617)
- [BG][S] Evron et al. (arXiv 2205.09588, COLT 2022) frame continual linear regression as successive projections onto task solution spaces and report that intermediate angles between consecutive tasks are most prone to forgetting after a single pass. — [arXiv 2205.09588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09588)
- [V-abs] Hiratani (arXiv 2405.20236, 30 May 2024; venue not listed on the arXiv page) separates input-feature similarity from readout similarity in a linear teacher-student model with latent structure: high input-feature similarity combined with low readout similarity is harmful for both knowledge transfer and retention, while the opposite combination is relatively benign. Results are consistent with a permuted-MNIST task with latent variables. — [arXiv 2405.20236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20236)
- [V-abs] Banayeeanzade, Soltanolkotabi and Rostami (arXiv 2408.16939, 29 August 2024, TMLR camera-ready) use overparameterized linear models to characterize how model size, dataset size and task similarity affect multi-task generalization and transfer, and how buffer size and model capacity affect the forgetting rate of replay-based continual learning; the abstract does not state the direction of the effects. — [arXiv 2408.16939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16939)

**Width, depth, and the feature-learning (rich) versus lazy (NTK-like) regime**
- [V-abs] Graldi, Breccia, Lanzillotta, Hofmann and Noci (arXiv 2506.16884, 20 June 2025, ICML 2025) use a variable parameterization to separate lazy and rich regimes and dynamical mean field theory for infinite-width networks in the feature-learning regime, extending earlier results limited to the lazy regime. Findings: increasing width is beneficial only when it reduces feature learning; high feature learning is beneficial only when tasks are highly similar; as task similarity changes the model moves from an effectively lazy, low-forgetting regime into a rich, high-forgetting regime; optimal performance sits at a critical, intermediate level of feature learning that depends on task non-stationarity and transfers across model scales. — [arXiv 2506.16884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16884)
- [V-abs] Guha and Lakshman (arXiv 2403.06398, 11 March 2024, ICML 2024) prove a direct relation between width and forgetting in feed-forward networks and conclude that widening to reduce forgetting yields diminishing returns, which they report observing empirically at widths not tested before. The abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv 2403.06398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06398)
- [V-abs] Masip, van de Ven, Ferrando and Tuytelaars (arXiv 2601.22012, 29 January 2026; v3 11 August 2026) model forgetting as changes to how individual features are encoded, which either reduce a feature's allocated capacity or disrupt its readout by downstream computation; their tractable toy model identifies best and worst cases and highlights a detrimental effect of depth. — [arXiv 2601.22012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22012)
- [S] "Interference and Retention in Continual Learning" (arXiv 2607.09202, July 2026) argues that in the frozen-feature regime the forgetting caused by a new task equals the interference energy it induces on the old task. — [arXiv 2607.09202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09202)

**Task order**
- [V-abs] Evron, Levinstein, Schliserman, Sherman, Koren, Soudry and Srebro (arXiv 2504.04579, 6 April 2025, accepted to ALT 2026) show that fitting a task in a continual linear model equals one SGD step on a modified objective. For jointly realizable regression tasks under random ordering with replacement they improve the previous best forgetting rate O((d - r̄)/k) to O(min(1/k^{1/4}, sqrt(d - r̄)/k, sqrt(T r̄)/k)) (d = dimension, r̄ = average task rank, T = number of tasks, k = iterations). Without replacement they give the first rate, O(min(1/T^{1/4}, (d - r̄)/T)), and state that "randomization alone, without task repetition, prevents catastrophic forgetting in sufficiently long task sequences." For linear classification on separable data they prove a matching O(1/k^{1/4}) rate. — [arXiv 2504.04579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04579)
- [V-abs] Li and Hiratani (arXiv 2502.03350, 5 February 2025; no venue on the arXiv page) derive two ordering principles from a linear teacher-student model with latent factors: order tasks "from the least representative to the most typical", and make adjacent tasks dissimilar. Following them improved task-incremental performance for MLPs and CNNs on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. — [arXiv 2502.03350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03350)
- [S] Ding, Ji, Wang and Xu (arXiv 2405.17583, May 2024, ICML 2024) analyze multi-step SGD on continual linear regression in both under- and over-parameterized regimes: with sufficient data, placing tasks whose population covariance has larger eigenvalues later tends to increase forgetting. — [arXiv 2405.17583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17583)
- [S] "From Order to Distribution: A Spectral Characterization of Forgetting in Continual Learning" (arXiv 2604.13460, April 2026) reframes forgetting, under a shared-solution assumption with i.i.d. tasks, as governed by the task-generating distribution rather than a particular ordering. — [arXiv 2604.13460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13460)

**Learning rate and optimizer**
- [S] Ding et al. (above) report that an appropriately chosen step size mitigates forgetting in both under- and over-parameterized regimes. — [arXiv 2405.17583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17583)
- [V-full] The 2024 review by van de Ven, Soures and Kudithipudi (arXiv 2403.05175, 8 March 2024, book chapter) states that standard optimizers were designed for stationary settings, that optimizer type affects continual-learning performance, that learning rate and batch size matter, and that the optimization routines that do best are those finding "wider or flatter minima" (attributed to Mirzadeh et al. 2020, background). — [arXiv 2403.05175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05175)
- [V-full] In a from-scratch Transformer on synthetic facts, Palit et al. (arXiv 2610.08718, 6 October 2026) find larger fine-tuning learning rates produce a deeper transient collapse of old-fact recall, and that the timing of the collapse's bottom tracks the learning of the new facts rather than the step count. — [arXiv 2610.08718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08718)
- [BG][S] Ashley, Ghiassian and Sutton (arXiv 2102.07686, 2021) found optimizer choice significantly changes forgetting, with SGD forgetting less than Adam. — [arXiv 2102.07686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07686); challenged by [S] Frati, Traft and Clune (arXiv 2507.01559, July 2025; convolutional networks), who report that optimizer choice deeply affects learning and forgetting dynamics and dispute the belief that SGD outperforms adaptive optimizers. — [arXiv 2507.01559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01559)
- [U] Dohare et al. (Nature, published 21 August 2024) report that standard deep-learning methods gradually lose plasticity under continual training until they learn no better than a shallow network, and that continual backpropagation (re-initializing a small fraction of less-used units) maintains plasticity. Not opened this session. — [Nature s41586-024-07711-7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7)

### Inferences
- For a tiny transformer trained from scratch (small width, feature-learning regime), the Graldi et al. result implies it sits on the "rich" side where forgetting is high unless tasks are very similar; a fixed-size baseline of larger width could forget less simply by being lazier, so width must be matched or reported when comparing against a growing model.
- "Intermediate similarity is worst" (Goldfarb et al.; Evron et al. 2022) plus "high feature similarity with low readout similarity is worst" (Hiratani) suggests a synthetic task sequence that reuses the same input tokens with conflicting outputs is close to a worst case, while disjoint inputs or shared input-output structure are comparatively benign.
- Because order provably matters (Ding; Li and Hiratani) while random long sequences forget less (Evron 2025), a small experiment should average over several task orderings and seeds rather than report one sequence.
- Learning rate is a confound in any forgetting comparison: step size changes forgetting in theory (Ding) and collapse depth in practice (Palit), so it should be held fixed or swept identically across the compared models.

### Gaps
- Almost all rigorous results are for linear models, teacher-student setups or infinite-width limits; I found no 2024-2026 theory specific to finite attention-based models trained from scratch on sequential tasks (the closest, arXiv 2605.28705, May 2026, concerns in-context continual learning within a prompt and was seen only via search summary).
- Depth: only the Masip et al. toy-model statement was verified; I did not verify a dedicated width-versus-depth theory paper for 2024-2026.
- Venue of Hiratani 2024 and Li and Hiratani 2025 not confirmed from the arXiv pages. Effect directions in Banayeeanzade et al. not stated in the abstract.
- No verified 2024-2026 study of warm-up, Adam versus SGD, or sharpness specifically for transformers in continual learning. The Frati et al. and Ashley et al. claims conflict and both were seen only via search summary.
- Leads seen by title only and not opened: arXiv 2610.14787 ("Formalizing Forgetting: Theory of Forgetting for Online Continual Learning"), arXiv 2604.17578 (recovery guarantees for dependent tasks), arXiv 2505.20970 (representational shifts as linear transformations).

## 2. What is the current evidence on the relative strength of replay, regularization, and parameter-isolation methods? Do simple baselines remain hard to beat?

### Takeaway
The verified 2024-2026 evidence supports a scenario-dependent ranking rather than a universal one: replay is regarded as important and perhaps necessary for class-incremental and large problems, parameter regularization works in task- and domain-incremental settings but struggles in class-incremental ones, and parameter isolation gives near-zero forgetting only when task identity is available and capacity lasts. Simple baselines (small replay, a frozen backbone with class prototypes, plain parameter-efficient fine-tuning, first-task adaptation) repeatedly match published state-of-the-art methods, and re-evaluations find most reported gains do not reproduce, but replay itself is not guaranteed to help.

### Cited Findings

**Family-level comparison (verified 2024 review)**
- [V-full] van de Ven, Soures and Kudithipudi (arXiv 2403.05175, 8 March 2024): replay "seems to play an important and perhaps necessary role" for large, complex problems; small amounts of replay can suffice. Parameter regularization (Fisher-based as in EWC, online importance as in SI) works in task- and domain-incremental settings, but such methods "often struggle to learn inter-task boundaries in class-incremental learning scenarios." Context-dependent processing / parameter isolation avoids forgetting but used alone is "limited to task-incremental learning type of problems"; using it otherwise requires a context-identification step that is itself a class-incremental problem. Template-based classification (prototypes, generative classifiers) helps class-incremental learning because it "avoids the need to make comparisons between classes" during training. Learning without Forgetting (functional regularization with current inputs as anchors) "tends to work well" when tasks share input structure. — [arXiv 2403.05175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05175)

**Replay: strong but not unconditionally**
- [V-abs] Mahdaviyeh, Lucas, Ren, Tolias, Zemel and Pitassi (arXiv 2506.04377, 4 June 2025, CoLLAs 2025) show in overparameterized continual linear regression that, even without noise, "forgetting can be non-monotonic with respect to the number of replay samples"; they construct worst-case task configurations where replay is harmful and distributional settings where replaying randomly chosen samples increases expected forgetting, and report similar harmful behavior in neural networks trained with SGD. Replay performance depends heavily on which samples are chosen and how related the tasks are. — [arXiv 2506.04377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04377)
- [V-abs] Wang, Chandra and Zhang (arXiv 2503.20018, 25 March 2025, preprint) report that an experience-replay buffer whose contents are processed by a Transformer eliminates loss of plasticity on regression, classification and policy-evaluation tasks without changing backpropagation, activations or regularization; they conjecture (not demonstrate) that in-context learning is the mechanism. — [arXiv 2503.20018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20018)
- [BG][S] Rolnick et al. (arXiv 1811.11682, NeurIPS 2019, CLEAR) found an equal mix of new and replayed experience a good stability-plasticity trade-off. — [arXiv 1811.11682](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)
- [BG][S] De Lange, van de Ven and Tuytelaars (arXiv 2205.13452, ICLR 2023): replay, constraint-based replay, distillation and regularization methods all show the "stability gap", a sharp transient drop in old-task performance when a new task starts. — [arXiv 2205.13452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13452)
- [S] For "spurious forgetting" in language models, Zheng et al. (arXiv 2501.13453, January 2025, ICLR 2025) report that freezing the bottom layers gave substantial improvements in four continual-learning scenarios, while replay-based, model-merging and gradient-modification approaches had limited success on that problem. — [arXiv 2501.13453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13453)

**Regularization (EWC, SI and successors)**
- [V-abs] van de Ven (arXiv 2502.11756, 17 February 2025, ICLR 2025 blog-post track): the exact Fisher Information computation for EWC is "rarely described" and several implementations circulate; many reported EWC results "could likely be improved by changing the way the Fisher Information is computed." — [arXiv 2502.11756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11756)
- [V-abs] Hiratani (arXiv 2405.20236, 30 May 2024): in the linear teacher-student model, weight regularization in the Fisher metric significantly improves retention regardless of task similarity without hurting transfer, but its diagonal approximation and Euclidean-space regularization are much less robust to task similarity. — [arXiv 2405.20236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20236)
- [V-abs] Thede, Roth, Hénaff, Bethge and Akata (arXiv 2406.09384, 13 June 2024, CoLLAs 2024) argue classic rehearsal-free techniques such as EWC and SI are worth reconsidering alongside recent pretrained-model methods. — [arXiv 2406.09384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09384)

**Parameter isolation (PackNet, HAT, supermasks, gating)**
- [V-abs] Hiratani 2024: task-dependent activity gating improves retention but reduces transfer; task-dependent plasticity gating has no effect on retention or transfer in the over-parameterized limit. — [arXiv 2405.20236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20236)
- [S] A 2025 paper (arXiv 2505.21942, May 2025, "Continual Learning Beyond Experience Rehearsal and Full Model Surrogates") notes parameter-isolation methods need task identity at inference and can exhaust capacity on long sequences, and reports PackNet and progressive networks comparable to or slightly above its own method in the task-incremental setting, which it attributes to their over-parameterization. — [arXiv 2505.21942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21942)
- [BG][S, secondary summary] De Lange et al. (arXiv 1909.08383, TPAMI 2021) found PackNet had the highest average accuracy on Tiny ImageNet in their task-incremental comparison, with iCaRL (replay) and MAS (regularization) competitive; PackNet's mask allocation explicitly limits the number of tasks. — [arXiv 1909.08383](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08383)
- [S] arXiv 2312.00840 (December 2023) argues magnitude-based sub-networks retain unimportant weights and claims 70% fewer sub-network parameters and 80% less training time than prior parameter-isolation work; numbers not verified against the paper. — [arXiv 2312.00840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00840)
- [BG][U] Foundational references, not opened this session: EWC (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017, [arXiv 1612.00796](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)); SI (Zenke et al. 2017, [arXiv 1703.04200](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04200)); PackNet (Mallya and Lazebnik 2018, [arXiv 1711.05769](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05769)); HAT (Serrà et al. 2018, [arXiv 1801.01423](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)); SupSup supermasks (Wortsman et al. 2020, [arXiv 2006.14769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14769)).

**Simple baselines and reproducibility**
- [V-abs] Cha and Cho (arXiv 2403.09066, 14 March 2024; TMLR 2025 camera-ready) show the standard protocol tunes hyperparameters on the same scenario it reports on. Under their two-phase protocol (tune on one dataset, freeze, evaluate on another with the same scenario configuration), across more than 8,000 class-incremental experiments with and without pretrained models, most state-of-the-art algorithms failed to reproduce their reported performance. — [arXiv 2403.09066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09066)
- [V-abs] Thede et al. (arXiv 2406.09384): methods with input-conditional query mechanisms do not benefit from them and collapse toward standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning shortcuts; a simple lightweight PEFT baseline can often match them; an implicit bound on tunable parameters may explain much of their effectiveness. — [arXiv 2406.09384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09384)
- [BG][S] Janson et al. (arXiv 2210.04428, 2022 workshop paper): a nearest-mean classifier on a frozen pretrained backbone reached 88.53% on 10-Split-CIFAR-100, surpassing most methods initialized from the same pretrained transformer. — [arXiv 2210.04428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04428)

### Inferences
- If the planned experiment supplies a task identifier to the model at test time, it is task-incremental: parameter isolation and regularization will look strong there, and a result should not be generalized to the class-incremental case, where the 2024 review says regularization struggles and replay is needed.
- A growing network is a form of parameter isolation; the evidence above says its fair comparators are (a) a fixed-size model with a small replay buffer and (b) a fixed-size model with the same final parameter count, since over-parameterization alone was offered as the explanation for PackNet and progressive-network strength.
- EWC should be reported with its exact Fisher computation, because the van de Ven 2025 result implies an EWC baseline can be made to look weak by implementation choice.
- Replay buffer contents and task relatedness should be reported; Mahdaviyeh et al. show replay can raise forgetting for some sample choices.

### Gaps
- I found no verified 2024-2026 head-to-head benchmark of PackNet, HAT or supermasks against replay and EWC on one protocol; the family ranking rests on a 2024 review, a 2021 survey seen through a secondary summary, and scattered single-paper baselines.
- "Joint training remains hard to beat" was not directly verified for 2024-2026. The 2024 review says only that practitioners often retrain from scratch on all data because "continued joint training often does not yield satisfactory results." Joint training is the standard reference in the intransigence metric (section 3).
- Small-buffer replay as a hard-to-beat baseline: supported qualitatively by the 2024 review ("small replay amounts can suffice") but I verified no 2024-2026 quantitative study. [U] Prabhu et al. (arXiv 2303.11165, CVPR 2023, compute-budgeted continual learning) and GDumb (ECCV 2020) are the usual citations for this and were not opened.
- Zheng et al.'s numerical results were not verified; an aggregator listed figures I could not confirm, so none are reported here.

## 3. What are the standard metrics and their exact definitions, and what evaluation pitfalls and benchmark critiques have been published?

### Takeaway
The standard metrics are all functions of one matrix a(k, j), the test accuracy on task j after training through task k: average accuracy, average incremental accuracy, forgetting measure, backward transfer, forward transfer and intransigence, with joint training and per-task from-scratch models as references. Published critiques concern end-only evaluation that hides transient collapse, tuning hyperparameters on the test scenario, pretrained backbones that make first-task adaptation and prototype baselines competitive, task-identity availability that makes task-incremental results uninformative about class-incremental learning, and two non-equivalent definitions of forward transfer.

### Cited Findings

**Definitions as given by Wang, Zhang, Su and Zhu, "A Comprehensive Survey of Continual Learning" (arXiv 2302.00487; 2023 per the ID) [V-full for the formulas; exact posting date and journal venue not shown on the page]** — [arXiv 2302.00487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00487)
- Notation: a(k, j) in [0, 1] is accuracy on the test set of task j after learning task k (j ≤ k).
- Average accuracy: AA_k = (1/k) Σ_{j=1..k} a(k, j).
- Average incremental accuracy: AIA_k = (1/k) Σ_{i=1..k} AA_i.
- Forgetting measure: f(j, k) = max over i in {1, ..., k-1} of (a(i, j) - a(k, j)) for j < k; FM_k = (1/(k-1)) Σ_{j=1..k-1} f(j, k).
- Backward transfer: BWT_k = (1/(k-1)) Σ_{j=1..k-1} (a(k, j) - a(j, j)); forgetting usually appears as negative BWT.
- Intransigence (the survey spells it "intransience"): IM_k = a*_k - a(k, k), where a*_k is the accuracy of a randomly initialized reference model jointly trained on the union of D_1..D_k.
- Forward transfer (survey version): FWT_k = (1/(k-1)) Σ_{j=2..k} (a(j, j) - ã_j), where ã_j is the accuracy of a randomly initialized reference model trained on D_j alone.

**Original GEM definitions (Lopez-Paz and Ranzato, arXiv 1706.08840, June 2017 per ID) [BG][V-full via ar5iv]** — [arXiv 1706.08840](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)
- R(i, j) is "the test classification accuracy of the model on task t_j after observing the last sample from task t_i"; b̄ is the vector of per-task test accuracies at random initialization.
- ACC = (1/T) Σ_{i=1..T} R(T, i). BWT = (1/(T-1)) Σ_{i=1..T-1} (R(T, i) - R(i, i)). FWT = (1/(T-1)) Σ_{i=2..T} (R(i-1, i) - b̄_i).
- The two forward-transfer definitions differ: GEM's measures zero-shot accuracy on a task before training on it relative to random initialization; the survey's measures accuracy after training on it relative to a from-scratch single-task model.

**Scenario definitions**
- [V-full] Wang et al.: domain-incremental = same label space, different input distributions, task identity not required; task-incremental = disjoint label spaces, task identity provided in training and testing; class-incremental = disjoint label spaces, task identity provided only in training; task-free = identity never provided; online = one-pass stream; also instance-incremental, blurred-boundary and continual pre-training. — [arXiv 2302.00487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00487)
- [V-full] van de Ven et al. 2024: in task-incremental learning the network always knows the task so task-specific components are possible and the challenge is to beat a separate-network-per-task baseline through positive transfer; class-incremental learning requires discriminating classes "not observed together"; the three scenarios are orthogonal to task-based versus task-free. — [arXiv 2403.05175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05175)
- [BG][U] The three-scenario taxonomy originates with van de Ven and Tolias 2019 ([arXiv 1904.07734](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)); not opened this session.

**Pitfalls and critiques**
- [V-full] Average accuracy alone hides dynamics: a model with "0% accuracy on the first task and 100% accuracy on the second" can match a rigid model's average. The review groups metrics into performance, diagnostics (learning accuracy as a plasticity measure, backward and forward transfer) and resource efficiency (compute, energy, memory growth), and notes that evaluation timing (end only versus periodic) matters. — [arXiv 2403.05175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05175)
- [BG][S] Stability gap: per-iteration evaluation reveals sharp temporary drops in old-task performance at task switches that end-of-task evaluation misses; the gap grows as tasks become more dissimilar; the authors propose worst-case metrics and note the computational cost of continual evaluation. — [arXiv 2205.13452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13452)
- [S] A follow-up (arXiv 2306.01904, June 2023) attributes the stability gap to high output-layer loss from class-distribution shift and to excessive plasticity. — [arXiv 2306.01904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01904)
- [V-abs] Hyperparameter leakage: tuning on the evaluated scenario overstates continual-learning ability (Cha and Cho; details in section 2). — [arXiv 2403.09066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09066)
- [V-abs] Pretrained-model confound: training only on the first task, or simple non-parametric baselines, can be competitive on rehearsal-free benchmarks; Thede et al. aim to separate true continual learning from first-task adaptation. — [arXiv 2406.09384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09384); see also [BG][S] Janson et al. — [arXiv 2210.04428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04428)
- [V-full] Task-identity availability: per-task heads or networks avoid forgetting only in task-incremental problems; otherwise identity must be inferred, which is itself class-incremental. — [arXiv 2403.05175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05175)
- [V-abs] Behavioral accuracy can understate retained knowledge: on sequential CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, accuracy on earlier tasks fell from 54.8% to 0% while linear probes retained about 76% of the original representational information (unreviewed preprint; details in section 5). — [arXiv 2606.06032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06032)

### Inferences
- Algebraic identity from the definitions: AA_T = (1/T) Σ_j a(j, j) + ((T-1)/T) · BWT_T, i.e. final average accuracy equals mean learning accuracy plus scaled backward transfer; reporting learning accuracy and BWT together therefore decomposes AA into plasticity and stability terms.
- FM_k ≥ -BWT_k always, because the maximum in f(j, k) includes i = j; the two coincide only if each task's best accuracy occurred right after it was learned.
- For the planned experiment: log the full a(k, j) matrix, evaluate all tasks at sub-task granularity around task switches (stability gap, transient collapse), state which forward-transfer definition is used, and train two references with the same architecture: joint training on all tasks (for intransigence) and from-scratch single-task models (for survey-style forward transfer).
- A 0% old-task accuracy as in the 2606.06032 result is characteristic of a single shared output head whose old classes are never predicted; I infer the setting is class-incremental, which the abstract does not state.
- Whether the tiny transformer receives a task token decides the scenario label; results should be reported as task-incremental if it does.

### Gaps
- "Learning accuracy" has no formula in the sources I opened; the usual definition (mean of a(j, j), from Riemer et al. 2019) is from my background knowledge and unverified here.
- The survey's attribution of each metric to original papers was returned only as reference numbers, so I could not confirm that forgetting and intransigence trace to Chaudhry et al. 2018 ([arXiv 1801.10112](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10112), [U]).
- Journal venue and date of the Wang et al. survey not confirmed from the page.
- I found no 2024-2026 paper specifically on task-identity leakage through input formatting in sequence models (for example a task token in the prompt); the critique above is the general task-incremental one.
- Other known critiques not verified this session: compute-budgeted evaluation, and surveys of pretrained-model continual learning.

## 4. What is known about forgetting specifically in transformers and in small models trained from scratch, including on synthetic or algorithmic task sequences?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin: I found no 2024-2026 study of a tiny transformer trained from scratch on a sequence of algorithmic tasks and compared with a fixed-size baseline. The closest controlled results use from-scratch transformers on synthetic fact recall or compositional reasoning and show that much of the observed forgetting is a transient, shared representational shift, that learned shortcut solutions block transfer and recovery, and that some recovery phenomena seen in large models need a minimum width and depth to appear.

### Cited Findings
- [V-full] Palit, Draye, Zucchet, Jin and Schölkopf (arXiv 2610.08718, 6 October 2026, preprint). From-scratch Transformer: 8 layers, model dimension 512, 8 heads, rotary position embeddings, pre-layer normalization, no MLP blocks, no dropout; parameter count not stated. Task: recall of synthetic biography facts (key = name plus attribute, value = answer; scored on first answer token). Pretraining: two populations of 2,000 individuals, 16,000 steps, batches of 256, to perfect recall; AdamW (β1 0.9, β2 0.95, weight decay 0.1, clipping at 1), cosine schedule from 5×10⁻⁴. Fine-tuning: 500 new individuals, peak learning rate 3.75×10⁻⁵, 1,200 steps, no replay. Result: when new answers are concentrated in one half of each attribute's values, old-fact recall collapses quickly, partially recovers as the new facts are learned without the old facts ever being seen again, then erodes slowly; when new answers span both halves there is gradual forgetting with no collapse. Subtracting the common part of the logit change removes the collapse, leaving slow erosion. Exact accuracies appear only in figures. — [arXiv 2610.08718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08718)
- [V-full] Same paper, mechanism: a minimal associative-memory model reproduces the pattern with keys sharing structure, concentrated new values, and RMS normalization; without normalization the shift is never withdrawn and the collapse is permanent. In the pretrained OLMo 2 1B, removing the top singular direction of each weight update in the Transformer blocks largely removes the collapse, a random rank-one control of equal norm does not, and a four-times-larger set of new individuals makes the collapse longer and deeper. Stated limitations: the minimal model is "deliberately extreme" and only one pretrained model was studied. — [arXiv 2610.08718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08718)
- [S] Zheng, Cai, Qiu and Ma (arXiv 2501.13453, January 2025, ICLR 2025) introduced "spurious forgetting" using controlled experiments on a synthesized dataset: early optimization steps on a new task disrupt previously established task alignment rather than erase knowledge, which they link theoretically to orthogonal weight updates; freezing bottom layers helps. — [arXiv 2501.13453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13453)
- [V-full] Yang, Jones, Mozer and Ren (arXiv 2403.09613, March 2024; NeurIPS 2024 per proceedings listing): for Pythia-style models trained from scratch, noticeable anticipatory recovery under cyclic training requires width of at least 512 and depth of at least 8 blocks; randomly initialized models show the effect more weakly than pretrained ones; average recovery jumps sharply between the 160M and 410M pretrained models. — [arXiv 2403.09613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09613)
- [V-abs] Redman, Johnson and Robinson (arXiv 2605.05495, 6 May 2026, preprint) extend the LEGO compositional-reasoning task to "continual LEGO". BERT learns a shortcut solution that limits generalization and prevents strong forward transfer; ALBERT (weight-shared, recurrent) shows a "For loop-esque" solution and does better; both fail when the task requires composition across experiences; ALBERT's drop is partly recovered by training on combined data, while BERT's shortcut is entrenched in initial training and does not recover. The abstract gives no model sizes, numbers, or statement on from-scratch training. — [arXiv 2605.05495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05495)
- [V-abs] Jain, Kirk, Lubana, Dick, Tanaka, Grefenstette, Rocktäschel and Krueger (arXiv 2311.12786, 21 November 2023; v2 21 August 2024; venue not on the arXiv page) study fine-tuning in synthetic, controlled settings plus TinyStories language models: fine-tuning rarely alters underlying capabilities, typically learns a minimal "wrapper" on top, and further fine-tuning on a task where the hidden capability is relevant revives it within a few gradient steps. — [arXiv 2311.12786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12786)
- [V-abs] Masip et al. (arXiv 2601.22012, January 2026) apply crosscoders to a Vision Transformer trained on sequential CIFAR-10 to track feature-level forgetting. — [arXiv 2601.22012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22012)
- [V-abs] Wang, Chandra and Zhang (arXiv 2503.20018, March 2025): Transformers processing replayed data avoid loss of plasticity on continual regression, classification and policy evaluation. — [arXiv 2503.20018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20018)
- [S] arXiv 2605.28705 (May 2026) studies in-context continual learning with GPT-2-style models on synthetic linear and two-layer ReLU regression tasks, attributing forgetting to attention reweighting rather than parameter overwriting; order sensitivity persists in linear self-attention without parameter interference. — [arXiv 2605.28705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28705)
- [S] A non-transformer comparison point: arXiv 2511.12828 (November 2025) trains Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks on five synthetic tasks in sequence and finds performance deteriorates more for tasks further back in time. — [arXiv 2511.12828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12828)

### Inferences
- A "tiny" transformer below roughly width 512 and depth 8 may not show anticipatory recovery at all (Yang et al.), so absence of recovery in the planned experiment would not contradict the large-model findings.
- Pre-layer normalization appears necessary for the collapse-then-recovery pattern (Palit et al.); whether the tiny transformer uses normalization, and where, is therefore a variable that changes the shape of forgetting curves and should be reported.
- The continual-LEGO result suggests that on algorithmic tasks a from-scratch transformer may fail to transfer because it learned a shortcut on the first task, which would show up as low forward transfer rather than as forgetting; both should be measured.
- Synthetic key-value or biography-style tasks with controllable answer overlap (Palit; Zheng) are an established controlled design that a small experiment could mirror to separate transient from permanent forgetting.

### Gaps
- No verified paper matches the planned design (tiny from-scratch transformer, sequence of small synthetic or algorithmic tasks, fixed-size versus growing comparison). Two targeted searches returned none; this is a real gap in what I could find, not proof that none exists.
- arXiv 2601.18699 ("Mechanistic Analysis of Catastrophic Forgetting in Large Language Models During Continual Fine-tuning", January 2026, single author) surfaced in search. Its abstract claims a 20-model study including closed-source models and an ambiguously worded headline figure (it "mitigates up to 94.2% of ancestral capabilities"); I judge it low reliability and have not used it. — [arXiv 2601.18699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18699)
- Model sizes and training-from-scratch status in the continual-LEGO paper, and the specific synthetic tasks in Jain et al., were not available at abstract level.
- The Graldi et al. (2506.16884) scaling results are for width and parameterization generally; I did not verify whether they include transformer experiments.

## 5. Are there 2025-2026 results suggesting forgetting is partly superficial?

### Takeaway
Yes: several independent 2024-2026 lines of evidence indicate that a large share of measured forgetting is loss of access or alignment rather than erasure, shown by recovery without re-exposure, recovery before re-exposure under cyclic training, revival within a few gradient steps, and linear-probe or classifier-only recovery. The strongest quantitative claims come from unreviewed 2026 preprints, and every source also reports a residual component that does not recover.

### Cited Findings
- [V-full] Anticipatory recovery (Yang, Jones, Mozer and Ren, arXiv 2403.09613, March 2024, NeurIPS 2024): when pretrained Pythia models are fine-tuned on documents in a fixed cyclic order, loss on a document rises after training on it and then falls again before the document recurs. Defaults: 25 documents, context length 256, 10 gradient steps per document at batch size 1, 5 epochs, vanilla gradient descent; main experiment 100 documents with Pythia-1B, where loss on document 1 peaks around document 60 and more than half of the initial forgetting is recovered before document 1 returns in epoch 2. Average online loss over epochs 2-5, cyclic versus shuffled: 410M 1.09 vs 1.34; 1B 1.03 vs 1.51; 1.4B 1.14 vs 1.51; 2.8B 1.34 vs 1.79. With random reshuffling each epoch there is no anticipatory recovery. Adam gives more recovery than vanilla gradient descent; more gradient steps increase it; clear recovery persists up to 200 documents; freezing more blocks lowers it; Image GPT, ViT and VGG-19 also show it, with language models strongest. The authors say they have not explained why autoregressive language models show it most. — [arXiv 2403.09613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09613)
- [S] Spurious forgetting (Zheng et al., arXiv 2501.13453, January 2025, ICLR 2025): the performance drop reflects a decline in task alignment rather than loss of underlying knowledge, and old-task performance can be retrieved if the alignment-undoing process is avoided. — [arXiv 2501.13453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13453)
- [V-full] Collapse, recovery, erosion (Palit et al., arXiv 2610.08718, 6 October 2026): forgetting decomposes into a shared, reversible loss of access caused by a common shift in old representations and a slow, permanent erosion of individual facts. In a pretrained model with real entities, removing one direction from each weight update restores most old facts that training never brought back, and the edited model recalls more old facts than the trained model at equal new-fact accuracy. The authors caution that normalization-driven recovery is likely weaker on real data. — [arXiv 2610.08718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08718)
- [V-abs] Wrappers and revival (Jain et al., arXiv 2311.12786, v2 August 2024): fine-tuning rarely alters underlying capabilities; a hidden capability is reused after only a few gradient steps of relevant fine-tuning. — [arXiv 2311.12786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12786)
- [V-abs] Implicit task inference (Kotha, Springer and Raghunathan, arXiv 2309.10105, 18 September 2023, ICLR 2024): fine-tuning skews a language model's implicit inference of which task a prompt requires rather than removing the capability; "conjugate prompting" (for example translating the prompt to another language) recovers in-context learning lost through instruction tuning, natural reasoning lost during code fine-tuning, and behavior suppressed by safety fine-tuning. — [arXiv 2309.10105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10105)
- [V-abs] Linear-probe and classifier-only recovery (Trivedi and Melwani, arXiv 2606.06032, 4 June 2026, unreviewed preprint): ResNet-18 on sequential CIFAR-100; accuracy on earlier tasks fell from 54.8% to 0%; linear probes retained about 76% of the original representational information; retraining only the final classifier restored 75.7% of original task performance without changing the backbone; early and intermediate layers kept highly recoverable information while later layers degraded severely. — [arXiv 2606.06032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06032)
- [V-abs] Concept-level recoverability (Filus, Faber, Corizzo and Kanan, arXiv 2605.16374, 10 May 2026, preprint): using sparse-autoencoder latents as concept proxies, a large portion of concept information that appears deleted is recoverable under a linearity assumption, but concept decodability degrades as more tasks are added. [S] The search summary of the full text adds that recovery is incomplete for all strategies except LwF, so degradation is not purely linear misalignment. — [arXiv 2605.16374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16374)
- [BG][S] The stability gap is itself a transient: old-task performance drops sharply at a task switch and then recovers during continued training, across replay, distillation and regularization methods. — [arXiv 2205.13452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13452)
- [S] Adjacent evidence from machine unlearning (arXiv 2505.16831, May 2025): representational alignment that is stable recovers after relearning, while aggressive unlearning causes irreversible degradation. — [arXiv 2505.16831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16831)

### Inferences
- These results imply accuracy-based forgetting can overstate knowledge loss, so the planned experiment should add at least two cheap measurements per old task: (a) relearning speed (steps to regain original accuracy, compared with learning from scratch) and (b) a linear probe or head-only retraining on frozen features.
- The recoverable component is consistently located in output or readout alignment (classifier-only recovery, a single update direction, wrappers), and the permanent component in fact- or feature-specific changes; a fixed-size model and a growing model could differ in the second while looking identical on the first, or the reverse, so reporting only final accuracy could mislead the comparison.
- Anticipatory recovery depends on a fixed cyclic order and disappears under reshuffling; a strictly one-pass task sequence cannot show it, but a design that revisits tasks cyclically could, subject to the width and depth threshold noted in section 4.
- Palit et al. describe recovery under continued training on new facts only, whereas Yang et al. describe recovery tied to cyclic re-exposure; the sources do not establish that these are one mechanism.

### Gaps
- The two papers with headline numbers for probe retention (2606.06032) and concept recoverability (2605.16374) are 2026 preprints with no peer-review status found; the first does not state its scenario. Their probe protocols differ and were not compared.
- I did not verify a peer-reviewed 2024-2026 study measuring relearning speed ("savings") directly in small from-scratch models; [BG][U] Davari et al. (CVPR 2022, [arXiv 2203.13381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13381)) is the usual background reference for linear-probe "representation forgetting" being smaller than observed forgetting and was not opened.
- Zheng et al. was confirmed only through search summaries of the ICLR listing; its quantitative results are not reported here.
- No source quantifies what fraction of forgetting is superficial in a way comparable across papers; the figures above (more than half recovered; about 76% probe retention; 75.7% classifier-only recovery) come from different models, tasks and definitions.
